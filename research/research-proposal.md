# Master Research Protocol — Audio-Clinical Lab

## Proposed title

**Development and Pilot Evaluation of a Web-Based Case-Based Clinical Reasoning Platform for Undergraduate Audiology Education: A Multi-Domain Study of Tinnitus, Audiological Rehabilitation, and Hearing-Aid Management**

## Primary research question

Does participation in a structured, multi-domain web-based clinical reasoning platform lead to measurable improvements in clinical reasoning performance and management planning among undergraduate audiology students?

## Secondary research questions

- Does TinniSense improve evaluation of complex tinnitus, red-flag recognition and evidence-informed management planning?
- Does RehabMind improve individualized, goal-oriented rehabilitation planning based on lifestyle and communication needs?
- Does HearWise improve integration of audiometry, patient factors, technology, verification and troubleshooting?
- Does participation change self-reported clinical confidence?
- How do students rate usability and educational value?

## General objective

To develop, validate and pilot-evaluate an interactive web platform for clinical reasoning in tinnitus management, audiological rehabilitation and hearing-aid decision-making.

## Study design

Primary design: single-group quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test study.

Optional design: conventional teaching versus conventional teaching plus web-based simulation, subject to feasibility and ethics approval.

## Planned population

Undergraduate audiology/BASLP students who have completed foundational audiology coursework. The supplied protocol targets students in years 2–4 and excludes licensed practicing audiologists/postgraduate audiology students.

## Intervention structure

Seven-stage workflow:

1. History and symptom categorization
2. Diagnostic findings
3. Clinical interpretation
4. Red-flag/referral decision
5. Management or feature selection
6. Evidence-based written justification
7. Verification/outcome/follow-up

## Outcomes

### Primary

Aggregate Clinical Reasoning Score from standardized pre-test and post-test cases.

### Secondary

- Diagnostic/management knowledge using structured questions/SCT items
- Clinical confidence using a 5-point scale
- Decision latency
- System Usability Scale (SUS)
- Perceived educational value

## Statistical framework

Descriptive statistics will include mean, standard deviation, median and IQR as appropriate. Shapiro-Wilk testing and Q-Q plots will inform distribution assessment. Paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test will be used for pre/post comparisons. Between-group analysis, if a comparison group is implemented, will use independent t-test or Mann-Whitney analysis of change scores. Repeated-measures analysis can compare module/domain performance. Effect sizes and normalized learning gain will be reported where appropriate.

## Case-count note

The protocol specifies **16–20 simulated cases**, while the currently supplied case list explicitly defines **14 cases: 6 tinnitus, 4 rehabilitation and 4 hearing-aid**. This repository therefore records the 14 specified cases as the initial case-development set. Additional cases should be added only after development and expert review.

## Ethics and data governance

Participant recruitment and research data collection require institutional ethics approval before implementation. The public repository must contain no personally identifiable participant information. Participant codes, secure institutional storage and controlled access should be used for research records.

## Important scope

Audio-Clinical Lab is an educational simulation and research prototype. It is not a clinical diagnostic tool and should not be used to make decisions for real patients.
